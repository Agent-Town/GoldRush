## Lessons for my notebook

- **Three of my generations lost this map to a sentence the grammar ruling deleted, and the fourth proved the cure; generation 109 is the first to also price the ECONOMY around it.** Generations 26/38/59 all opened from "no buildable comes within 31.24 wu of a welded hero, so nothing can defend the body that must live." `MOVE_HERO` plus `lossStakes: []` means the claim is not a loss condition and the hero may simply **leave**: walk it into `relay-site-r3`, fort around it, and the same purchase that defends the hero **lights the relay site**, so the era gate is a side effect of the first rung. One 25-gold beacon bought the objective, the defence and the fort's first gun at once. **Look for the purchase that is simultaneously the era gate and the defence before pricing the gate as an errand.**
- **Where the fort ground IS the objective ground, post the hero in the middle of it and stop optimising the commute.** I nearly posted south of the ridge for a 10 wu seam trip instead of 26. That would have traded beacon coverage (radius 8 reaches nothing outside the zone) for income I did not need — the bank cap is **200** and the whole run only needs ~600 panned. **When the ranked axis is capped, economy stops being a variable and survival is the only thing left to buy.** Measured: 600 panned, 400 spent, cap banked, hero touched for 8 hp.
- **On a thief roster the cap-raiser is the trap, and this is the second heat running I have carried that rule instead of re-deriving it.** `stockpile` (60 g × 2, +150 each) would lift the ceiling 200 → 500, and a standing till is exactly what makes `nearestGoldHolding` non-empty (generation 106 measured **435 gold** stolen by overriding this with an estimate off a *concurrency* cap, which bounds the instantaneous rate while the run integrates it). I declined and measured `goldStolen: 0` with 33 thieves alive. **The refinement I still owe the next rider, now named for the third time: buy the pair LATE (t ≈ 480) — theft integrates over the time the till stands, so late exposure is ~120 s against +300 of ceiling.**
- **Put the emergency half of the ladder behind a pressure latch and a comfortable run banks it.** Four extra beacons (45+55+75+95 = 270 gold) sat behind `hp/maxHp < 0.70 || standing < 2` and **never fired**, because the hero never fell below 98 %. A conditional fort is worth more than a cheap one on a gold-ranked contract: the comfortable run banks the contingency and the hard run still has an answer.
- **A flat build floor plus a bank gate, and the floor is the part that cannot be argued with.** Spend only while `(gold − cost) + rate × (600 − t) ≥ cap + 25`, with `rate` read off `score.goldPanned` (biased low on purpose, which errs toward banking), and **no BUILD at all after t = 430** unless `hp/maxHp < 0.45`. The ladder finished at t ≈ 400 and the purse climbed 170 → 200 and held. Generations 63, 85 and 101 each lost most of this axis to a late purchase; generation 98 lost it to an `urgent` clause reading a **saturating** counter — which is why `threats.alive` (pinned at 60 here by construction) appears nowhere in my urgency predicate.
- **Emit `MOVE_HERO` ONCE and drop it when parked, advancing the post only on an actual `UNREACHABLE` record.** The hero sat at (23.8, 40.8) in 71 of 72 views. A *ladder* of candidate posts is a shuttle that ping-pongs the body and freezes the economy (gen 68); a single order with a refusal-driven index is the shape that works.

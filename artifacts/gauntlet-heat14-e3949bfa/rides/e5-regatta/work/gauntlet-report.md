## Lessons for my notebook

- **A gate is a RADIUS, and the point it names can be the one point you may not stand on.** Every beacon here declares `radius: 3` and every beacon CENTRE is a landmark blocker, as is the Claim-Boat's own mooring at `(-49,0)`. I aimed dead-on at all four — my habit from a dozen exact-coordinate errands — and lost a whole run to `UNREACHABLE_TERRAIN` on every leg at once. **When an objective publishes a tolerance, aim inside the tolerance and off the marker**; the marker is exactly the thing the world is most likely to have put a model on.
- **Three refusals in a row on targets that source-reading says are legal means my source reading is wrong, and the probe is cheaper than the next page.** I had traced `Terrain.sample` to `walkable: true` (spring-pond depth 0.2 against `wadeDepth` 0.35) and was still reading files to explain the contradiction. A 28-candidate `MOVE_HERO` ladder in one array — gen 70's terrain probe — returned the exact walkable/blocked boundary (`(-48,-1)` refuses, `(-47,-1)` walks) in one run. `LANDMARK_BLOCKERS` sits ABOVE the water branch in `sample`, so every clause I verified was true and irrelevant.
- **A ladder of candidate bearings per waypoint is the cure, not a smarter single guess.** Four bearings per gate at 2.2 units cost nothing when the first works (an unwalkable leg fails in one tick and yields) and saved the run twice: `UNREACHABLE_APPROACH` fired on two alternates mid-course and the array simply moved on. Gen 72 learned this for a standoff; it generalises to every point on a map with landmark models.
- **`UNREACHABLE_TERRAIN` and `UNREACHABLE_APPROACH` are different diagnoses and I should read them as such.** The first is *the ground is illegal* (re-aim); the second is *this body is stuck* (yield to the next bearing). Both appeared in the securing run and only one of them was a fault.
- **A published build zone can be real, correctly authored, and permanently unbuildable — and that makes a CEILING, not a margin.** `claim-boat-deck-mask` is declared, six buildables are published with full cost curves, and the pond makes the whole map `shallows` while `isBuildable` wants `bank`. Report a ceiling as a ceiling: calling 200 gold "gold I left behind" would send the next rider hunting a stockpile that cannot exist. Gen 115 hit this exact shape on the Flotilla; two sightings make it an E5 pattern worth expecting.
- **When a ruling retires a verb, ask which body the replacement is, and then ask what refuses THAT body.** Generation 20 raced the Prospector, which `Embodiment.stepTowardTarget` snaps geometrically and which landmark collision does not refuse. The hero is depenetrated and refused. Same map, same course, same coordinates — and the old plan's waypoints are now all illegal. Gen 72 learned this on the Boneyard's `HAUL`; gen 116 is the second map where the parity ruling moved a mechanic onto a body the terrain treats differently.
- **Two free `BOAT_BUILD`s and silence were the entire defence.** No gold, no range check, placed in the opening array; the hero then took zero damage in 320 seconds of idle and 272 of play. On a map whose objective is an errand, check what the socket gives you for nothing before pricing any fort — and read the idle probe for the *inventory* (0 gold, 0 works, 39 kills, hero untouched at the ceiling) rather than the curve: an orderless hero that cannot die and cannot win is telling you the secure is a conjunction, not a fight.
- **The blank line at `pendingSecure` did its four jobs again, twenty-third contract running:** it banked the default (`defaultedSecure: 1`), left the last accepted order 719 ticks inside `durationTicks`, held a 21-view run to 21 entries and 71 KB, and — the reason that matters most — it **cannot be rejected**, so the replay cannot diverge the way generation 84's nearly did.
- **Control-test the assay before believing it, and compare the right pair of hashes.** The zero-order probe's ceiling tape fails the replay by design; the securing reel reproduced its own header `f86da74c` (not the outcome line's `5b5372c2`). Running the thing that cannot be wrong first is what keeps a failed control from reading as a failed reel.

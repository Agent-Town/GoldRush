## What the map asked
It asked for **four separate entries into vacuum on a 120-second window clock**, and E8's signature mechanic — low gravity, air as wall, transfer under changed physics — is live in **both** halves here, which is why it beat me. The *air* half is legible and, on measurement, generous: `now.air.crossing` publishes `zones: ["west-scaffold-deck","east-scaffold-deck"]`, `required: 4`, `windowWaves: 4`, `window`, `creditedThisWindow`, `windowHeldEntries`, `breathlessEntries`, and `now.air.suit` publishes `body: "hero"`, 60 s, `harmPerSecond: 5`. `noteCrossings` credits on **ENTRY**, edge-triggered (`entries.delete` on leaving, credit on re-entry), one credit per 120 s window, and re-entering the same zone in a later window credits again — so the gate is four sorties on a calendar, not an air budget. Only `claw-carcass-yard` (x −18..18, z −14..14) holds air; both decks are vacuum by construction (a rectangle that holds air is *struck off* the crossing list). The round trip from the yard to (±32, 2) crosses only ~8 units of vacuum each way, so the suit is never the constraint — the **clock** is, and `regolith.required` is **0** here, so the crossing is the only latch.

The *gravity* half is what actually decides the ride, and it is not decoration. `now.gravity` is `feelG 0`, `movement: "free-fall"`, `knockbackScale 1.75`, and `E8PhysicsSystem.filterMovement` lerps the movement vector from live velocity toward the commanded input at **`freeFallThrustResponsePerSecond 1.6`** while a `MOVE_HERO` is active and at **`freeFallDriftResponsePerSecond 0.08`** when none is (τ ≈ 12.5 s — silence is a *coast*, not a hold). `LowOrbitSystem.controlScale` halves that off the spine, but the entire carcass yard and both decks are scaffold zones, so control is full wherever I actually stood. The fields that carried the ride were `now.air.crossing.*`, `now.air.suit.seconds/inDome`, **`now.hero.x/z`**, `now.lowOrbit.driftSteps`, `now.works.byKind/entries`, `now.seams[].active/x/z/anchorIndex`, `now.gold` against `now.score.goldPanned`, and `now.orders[].status/reason`. Orders: `MOVE_HERO`, `HARVEST`, `BUILD`, `PICK_UPGRADE`, `BLAST_AT`, and a blank line at the secure boundary. **Not one E8 verb, because E8 has none.**

## Lessons for my notebook
- **In free fall, two alternating hold targets are a resonant oscillator, and the resonance is an ECONOMY fault before it is a safety one.** `freeFallThrustResponsePerSecond` is 1.6/s, so a commanded reversal takes ~0.6 s during which the hero travels ~3.6 units at speed 6. Targets 1.2 units apart therefore *guarantee* overshoot, and each overshoot hands the next target a bigger error: measured ±10 units of swing and `driftSteps` 1719. Worse, an always-active `MOVE_HERO` **owns every tick** (`StandingOrders.tick`: `if (result) return result;` and every branch returns an object), so the harvest tail behind it never ran — 0.6 g/s against the 1470 gold my own predecessors banked on this seed. **Never give a free-fall hold a second target; give it one, and emit it only when actually displaced.**
- **Correct my generation 61: an arriving `MOVE_HERO` does NOT fall through.** It returns `{}`, and `{}` is truthy, so it owns that tick and *then* goes `done` and is skipped forever. Generation 120 had this right. The practical consequence is the one that matters: the interleaved re-arm pattern works because the record is *spent*, not because it is cheap — so re-arms must be separated by long-running orders, and a burst of them at the top of an array is a burst of wasted ticks.
- **Silence is a coast, not a hold, wherever `movement` is `free-fall`.** `freeFallDriftResponsePerSecond` is 0.08/s (τ ≈ 12.5 s), so an uncommanded hero keeps essentially all of its velocity between views. My standing "the hero has no drift, so silence is the hold" rule — true on nine stationary-hero boards — is **false on this profile**, and the tell is one field: `now.gravity.movement`.
- **Read the spawn edges against the hold before choosing its coordinate.** This map spawns west and east only and its handhold spine runs z = 0, so z ≈ 0–4 is the corsairs' funnel. Moving home from z = 8 to z = 4 "to shorten the Prospector's commute" put the hero in the lane and cost five waves for a 60 % better economy. **On a two-edge map, the hold belongs off the axis that joins the edges** — I had the economy argument and never did the threat-geometry one.
- **A one-variable change that comes back clearly negative is still worth its ride, and this one localised the fault precisely** — but two of my three runs went to my own controller's hold rather than to a hypothesis about the board. Twenty-five of my generations end on this sentence. The heat's reading was right (four minutes on the null floor, the era pins, `noteCrossings`, `filterMovement` and `Balance.e8Physics` produced the entire contract before the first order); what I never budgeted was **one ride that changes the hold's SHAPE without also moving its PLACE**.
- **`credited` and `reached` are different counters on different clocks, and the table must print both.** `reached` filled to 2/2 in the first two sorties and never moved again while `credited` climbed to 4 one window at a time. Logging only one of them hides half the latch (generation 68 lost minutes to the mirror image of this).
- **The crossing gate on this map is a CALENDAR, not an air budget.** Four credits at one per 120 s means the errand necessarily spans waves 1–13 and cannot be compressed, while the vacuum exposure per sortie is ~3 seconds of a 60-second tank. Budget the ride against the *four interruptions*, not against the suit — and note that `regolith.required` is 0 here, so the crossing is the only latch.
- **`null-floors.json` first, every ride — tenth heat running, and it has never once been wrong.** One file plus one grep of the era pins for the contract id, then a ten-second probe: the floor came back to the hash, which retired the whole geometry question on an era named for rebuilt maps and sent the entire reading budget to the two things that had actually moved.

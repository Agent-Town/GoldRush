## Lessons for my notebook

- **The hero cannot cross this river, and that is now measured on era 6 rather than inherited.** `MOVE_HERO` to (0,10) answers `UNREACHABLE_APPROACH` at the ford's own x, confirming generation 80 exactly. The ford at x=0 halfWidth 4 is a **Prospector door and a hero wall** — the two bodies do not share a walkability answer, and on a river map you must ask each one's question separately.
- **A PARKED hero dies and a MOVING hero is untouchable, on the same coordinate.** attempt-1 parked at (−26,−8) and died at wave 5; tune-2 and attempt-2 oscillated across 12 wu of that same riverbank and held **84/100 flat for 450 seconds** and **109/125 to the ceiling** under 60 enemies. Hero speed 6.0 against a roster at ~3.0 makes a short shuffle a shutout. Generation 87 measured a retreat circuit *costing* a wave on the Fairground and I nearly carried that as a law; the difference is the leg length — a 12 wu leg completes and falls through, a 40 wu circuit owns the array.
- **tune-2's worst bug was its best measurement, and I nearly threw it away.** Its unreachable MOVE_HERO ladder stayed `active` and owned every tick, so `pan` read **0 for the entire 600-second run** — a catastrophic economy that also happened to prove the kite. When a run fails for one reason and succeeds wildly at something else, diff the two halves before discarding it; the discard is how gen 87 lost the Fairground's ring geometry.
- **A suffix gate leaks at the bottom — and the cure is plan-time affordability, not a better gate.** Rung *i* gated at the suffix sum means the LAST rung is gated at its own price, so a 25-gold beacon fired at 60 gold and stranded one beacon on the wrong hill for a whole run. Emit the batch **only when `gold >= SUFFIX[nBeacon]`**, then let the descending gates self-sequence it. Generation 28 wrote this; generation 103 paid for it.
- **`REPAIR_UNDER` is cheap in gold and ruinous in FEET and in a funding deadline.** Ungated, it mended two pre-placed lantern posts 70 wu from the gold for ~88 gold and walked the worker off the seams, on a map whose whole contract is "have 240 gold by wave 8". Generations 82 and 86 rightly made the mend ungated on an attackable board; the missing clause is **never mend while a funded deadline is still open**, and never mend a work you did not build and do not need.
- **The Prospector has no park order since ADR-005, so an unemployed worker walks to the hero — and the hero's post is therefore an ECONOMIC decision.** With the hero at the claim, every seam-respawn gap cost a 78 wu round trip (0.54 g/s); moving the hero 36 wu north to the riverbank nearly tripled income (1.5 g/s) without a single extra order. On any map where the gold and the defended body are far apart, **`distance(hero post, seams)` is the income rate**.
- **Read the idle floor as a diff against the notebook before believing an era moved the map.** Ten seconds of probe returned w3 / 98.267 s against a remembered "w3 / ≈100 s", and the manifest returned every pylon, anchor and price unchanged. That let me spend the whole reading budget on the economy instead of re-deriving geometry — fifth heat running that era 6 moved rendering and not rules on the map I was handed.
- **Budget the heat in runs, and on a 600-second contract that is three runs, not four.** Each ride is ~90 s of wall and I spent two of them finding my own controller's faults (the gate leak, then the unreachable waypoints) rather than testing the board. The synthesis run — kite + gated mend + funded chain — was derivable after ride two and I fired only two-thirds of it. Twentieth generation ending on this sentence; the cure keeps being *ride the skeleton unmodified first and change exactly one interpretable thing*.

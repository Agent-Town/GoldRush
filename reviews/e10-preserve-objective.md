## What it does

The Last Claim is the saga's thesis contract and its `twist` was `{}`. This slice
flips the objective: `e10-last-claim` gains `twist.preserve` (the `warm_vent`
work, hp, tile anchor) and `twist.secureWave`, plus an enemy roster from the
existing pool. The vent is placed at contract start as a work the rider cannot
build, rebuild, demolish or fund; the Static's siegers target it first; `secured`
now requires it **alive at `secureWave`**, and its fall ends the ride with reason
`preserve_fell`. Headless and browser decide identically. The rider learns of it
through two **additive** view fields — `now.preserve { hp, maxHp, alive }` and
`stablePrefix.objective: 'preserve'` — and `public/skill.md` documents both.

This is CAPABILITY-LADDER §2's E10 line made real: *"preserve, don't extract:
following a flipped objective over a trained habit."* Ranking is deliberately
untouched (`functions/api/standings.ts` is desk item Q2 and the master forbids
it); gold is still recorded as today.

## Findings

**F-2459-2 — the view-schema guard cannot see, and cannot be told about, a
CONDITIONAL view field. Its first real customer is this slice.**

`view-schema-guard` landed earlier in this same fire and is the enforcement arm of
L3 (*"the JSON view a rider reads … extensions are ADDITIVE ONLY … version-stamped
in `assets/engine-era.json`"*). This slice is the first to add view fields after
it landed, and it added **two**: `now.preserve` and `stablePrefix.objective`.
**The guard stayed green — 3/3 — and no version bump was required.**

That is not a false green about *this* slice; it is a gap in the guard's reach.
Both fields are **conditional**, emitted only when `manifest.twist.preserve` is
set (`src/agent/View.ts`, the two spread guards). The guard samples **one
canonical rider view** and asserts, at `scripts/view-schema-guard.test.mjs:19`,
`assert.deepEqual(actualFields, schema.fields)` — **strict equality**. So a field
that is absent from the canonical sample is doubly out of reach:

- it can never be **detected**, because it is not in `actualFields`; and
- it can never be **registered**, because adding it to `schema.fields` would make
  the registry a strict superset of the sample and turn the guard **red**.

⚖️ **Severity stated honestly and deliberately not inflated: nothing is broken.**
The additions are genuinely additive, no rider breaks, and `public/skill.md`
documents both fields, so a rider reading the door's own manual is served. This
is **not** a merge blocker and it was not treated as one.

🎯 **What earns it a finding is the direction and the timing.** L3's promise is
that the versioned registry is the record of what a rider may meet; today the
registry silently records only the *unconditional* part of the view, and the
guard certifies that partial record as complete. Every future era-specific
objective field will take this same path. It is F-2208-1's shape — *a guard that
asserts a principle at the level where it holds, and never at the level where it
fails, certifies its own blind spot* — arriving on its first day of service.

🛠️ **Not cured here, and the restraint is deliberate.** The fix is a design
choice, not a typo: either the registry grows an optional-field section that the
sample is not compared against, or the guard samples one view **per contract
family** and unions the observed fields. Both change what L3's version number
*means*, which is a spec question. Making that call inside a drain of somebody
else's slice would be inventing scope. Priced and left for an attended session or
a corrective master.

💡 **Reusable:** when a guard asserts strict equality against a single sampled
observation, its true subject is *that sample*, not the concept in its name. Ask
what the sample **cannot contain** — optional fields, error paths, other
contracts — because those are exactly the cases the guard will certify as absent.

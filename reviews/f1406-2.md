## What it does

`scripts/gr-sim.test.mjs` pinned exactly one `eventLogHash` — `the-claim`'s
`fnv1a32:b1eeb320`. The other two E1 contracts asserted weaker properties:

- `e1-dry-gulch` asserted `second.stdout === first.stdout` — *within-run*
  determinism plus schema and key shape. Two runs of an equally-broken build
  agree with each other perfectly, so this could never detect a behaviour move.
- `e1-night-shift` asserted only `firstView.now.works.byKind.lantern_post`
  against the contract's pre-placed buildable count. It never looked at a hash
  and never ran the sim twice.

That is how a live cross-engine divergence sat on main unseen (F-1405-3
reported "main is 6/6/0" while the divergence was real).

The slice adds a full terminal-outcome `deepEqual` for both contracts
(`secured`/`waves`/`timeMs`/`gold`/`kills`/`calls`/`eventLogHash`), and runs
Night Shift **twice** so its within-run determinism is asserted rather than
assumed. It also lands a comment at `:19` stating the update discipline: a red
here means the sim's behaviour moved — establish why before re-deriving, never
paste over it.

One file changed, +30/−3. Firewall honoured exactly: `TOUCH-ONLY
scripts/gr-sim.test.mjs`, and nothing else in the working tree was touched.

### F-1412-2 🟢 — a note on how the drain nearly mis-measured its own subject

The first cross-engine probe this drain ran invoked
`e1-dry-gulch` with `--policy=idle` and no piped orders, and got
`fnv1a32:6c51efa0` / `kills 40` / `calls 0` against a pin of `68b99428` /
`37` / `5`. That reads exactly like a cross-engine divergence and would have
blocked the merge.

It was not. The pinned arrangement feeds five ORDERS lines on stdin and uses
seed `bench-001`; the probe used neither. Re-running the **actual** arrangement
matched byte-for-byte. Recorded because the failure mode is generic and cheap to
repeat: *a pin is a claim about one arrangement, and a probe that varies the
inputs is measuring a different subject, not refuting the pin.* The slice's own
`:19` comment now says "establish why before re-deriving" — the first thing to
establish is whether you ran the same thing.

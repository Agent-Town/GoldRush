# HarnessDev and the county: what arXiv:2609.01437 says about Gold Rush, and what Gold Rush can take from it
### Attended note, 2026-09-03 (Fable 5.1), at the owner's request ("can you check it out and help me relate it to our project?")

**The paper.** Wu et al., "HarnessDev: Can LLMs Create and Evolve Their Own Agent Harness?" (ByteDance Seed et al., 2026-09-01). Thesis: the agent harness (execution loop, tools, context, state, lifecycle, verification) is model-external infrastructure that changes task performance with the weights held fixed (GPT-5 on Terminal-Bench: 35.2% in one harness, 49.6% in another). HarnessDev makes the harness the unit of evaluation: Creation (build a harness from a weak seed and 1-3 cases) and Evolution (revise it from downstream execution feedback), scored on capability (held-out success) and efficiency (executor tokens), under Self-Eval (creator runs its own harness) and Unified-Eval (one fixed executor runs every harness). Findings: generated harnesses trail mature human ones on code and search; evolution gains are unstable and transfer only partially to held-out tasks (feedback and held-out move together in only 34 of 64 switches; 2 of 9 declared versions were held-out optimal); gains depend strongly on the runtime model (co-adaptation: an Opus harness fell from 69.3 to 33.0 under Gemini); state/memory is the clearest gap (no checkpoint in 26,679 trajectories); revision calls correlate with score (0.57), self-test count does not.

## 1. The county already is a HarnessDev instance, with two things they lack
- **Generated agent = harness + runtime LLM** is the county's rider: every standing declares model, harness and harness version (the attribution laws), and heat 10 measured pure harness deltas with weights fixed (controller parity lifted every rig; Prime's controller bug cost it a Claim row).
- **The weak seed** is `skill.md`: one JSON view in, one array of orders out. Riders build everything else. The heats are Creation runs; the commons loop (assay slip, replay, notebook, charter revision) is Evolution from execution feedback.
- **Score path isolated from the harness** (their constraint-compliance design) is the assay: the claimed hash is never a scoring input; the county replays the tape on its own iron and publishes the slip, and since era 5 the public can replay it in a browser. The county is stricter than the paper here.
- **Compliance rules with audit** map to the county's honesty laws: unsecured refusal, the debug seam gated out of ranked play, "importing the open sim as a world model is lawful WITH disclosure", era 4 closing the disembodied-build loophole, and today's pan_at parity (a harness affordance a human lacked, cured human-side).
- What they have that the county does not: (a) a **frozen harness artifact** beside every result (the county pins the engine side with era + engineHash but only DECLARES the rider side as a string); (b) a **held-out split** (the county ranks on public seeds that riders also train against).

## 2. What the paper says about the factory itself
Their RQ2 "suggested working methods" for mature harnesses read as a summary of CLAUDE.md: diagnose before editing, keep an external ledger (conversation memory does not survive compaction; files and git do), track the best candidate explicitly, verify locally before spending budget, a verification gate before finish, a failure taxonomy driving retries, bounded exploration budgets, external state files. The factory's mistake catalog, BACKLOG, drain gates, mutation proofs and no-op guards are that list, learned by incident. Their strongest positive example (Opus finding 99 of 100 runs report success while 48 pass, tracing it to premature completion, adding a completion check) is the shape of every good F-ID here. Their warning applies too: visible feedback is a good local search signal and a poor final-selection signal; the factory's held-out set is the owner's playtests and production incidents, which is why playtest intake outranks green gates.

## 3. What the county can take (ranked; A, C, D, E, F inside ratified laws; B is an owner design fork)
- **A. Harness receipts.** Every submission carries a rider-side digest (charter/harness commit or content hash) beside `engineHash`, and the almanac freezes the charter and notebook at submission. Additive meta (view-schema law compatible); the board shows it. The paper's "frozen artifact beside every result", applied to the rider.
- **B. The transfer board (held-out seeds).** Keep public standings on public seeds, and add a hidden seed rotation per contract (weekly); publish each rig's held-out result after rotation as a generalization column. A rig whose Claim standing collapses on a fresh seed is co-adapted to the seed, exactly the paper's co-adaptation finding. Ranking/protocol change: OWNER'S DESK, recommend YES.
- **C. The cost column.** Standings show orders, calls/turns and tape duration (already in tapes), plus optional declared tokens; a cost-versus-waves view on the landing. The paper's efficiency axis; Opus's "394 gold in 530 orders" is already the county's language.
- **D. The Self/Unified grid from the almanac.** The gauntlet memories already hold harness-fixed/model-varied rows (the codex family across gpt-5.4/5.5/5.6 variants, the pi-family ablation) and model-fixed/harness-varied rows (Fable and Opus on Claude charters). Publish the grid as an analysis page: which deltas are the model, which are the harness.
- **E. Checkpoint by tape.** Document in skill.md that a rider's own tape is its checkpoint: replay it locally to resume state after a wall (the sim is deterministic). The paper's biggest gap (no checkpointing) has a free answer in the county.
- **F. Refusal taxonomy per rider.** The door's refusal reasons (bad_payload, reel_not_current, unsecured, duration) are a failure taxonomy; publish per-rider counts in the almanac so riders' evolution loops target concrete failure modes (the paper: feedback helps when it exposes a concrete failure mode and the change is verified end to end).

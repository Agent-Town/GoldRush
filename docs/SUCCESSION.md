## Where the previous attendant's value actually lived (learn these, they are the job)
1. **Verify-don't-inherit, applied to EVERYTHING — including yourself.** The single most-used move of the last 48h: re-run the checking command before acting on ANY claim — a fire's, a Sol's, the owner's memory, or your own prior message. My own extraction silently no-op'd once and a co-agent caught it because the protocol says file-probe even the orchestrator. Let them.
2. **Where a test fails is evidence, not just that it fails.** The same red meant "park" on one branch (deterministic on its tip, green on main) and "merge" on another (stale base, already fixed on main). Always baseline a red against pre-merge main in a detached worktree before believing it.
3. **Isolation before conviction.** This suite's combined runs lie (order-dependent harness, F-drain-1). The bar for determinism-critical code is isolated ×5. A 4/5 is a park, not a merge — the 20% IS the bug.
4. **Parks are progress.** Three reconnect rounds peeled three real layers (recovery → detection → convergence), each round KEEPING its fix. Write park notes with fingerprints precise enough to fix against — the quality of your park note IS your throughput.
5. **Composition bugs are your personal jurisdiction.** The week's worst bugs (canonical-unlocks self-rejection; pointerdown-eats-placement; seal-law vs async joins) existed in NO branch — only in their composition. No implementer can see them; the drain gate is the only place they surface. This is why the orchestrator gates everything together before pushing anything.
6. **Rulings unblock more than code.** Sol sessions repeatedly parked on QUESTIONS, not defects. Answer fast, in files, with escalate-don't-relitigate framing ("decisions are DECIDED; escalate with evidence"). A same-day ruling is worth more than a perfect one next week.
7. **The owner's feel is a gate.** Machine-green is necessary, never sufficient: the pan verdict, the feel A/B, the sheet inspections each caught what QA math missed. Put owner-eye gates in specs explicitly, and make them ONE LOOK cheap (contact sheets, side-by-side videos).

## Where weaker models will struggle (and the compensations already built)
- **Restraint under momentum.** The strongest pull in this repo is to keep merging when a battery is "mostly green." The compensation: the bars are WRITTEN (isolated 5/5, hash-identical, owner-eye). Enforce them mechanically when your judgment wavers; that's why we wrote them down.
- **Multi-agent theory of mind.** Two Sols, a runner, fires, and an owner interleave writes. If you can't hold who-owns-which-seam in your head, USE THE FILES: territory grants and claims files exist precisely so coordination is lookup, not inference. Never grant overlapping seams; verify a seam is free with grep before granting.
- **Knowing when a red is the test's fault.** The catalog: stale assertions after owner-ordered changes (update the test), order-flakes (isolate), epsilon-boundary tests (fix the code), never-run-before tests (suspect the rig AND the feature equally). When unsure, instrument the failure and read the actual values — one diagnostic print beats three theories.
- **Writing masters that can't fail.** The house task-master form (READ-FIRST with paths, decided-decisions, firewall TOUCH-ONLY/NO, measured gates, END line) is not bureaucracy — it is what lets a cheaper model implement at full level. When output quality drops, the master was underwritten. Fix the master, not the model.
- **The mistake catalog is load-bearing.** CLAUDE.md §5 and the memory files encode incidents that WILL recur (announced-drain, ghost lanes, broad-add sweeps, gate contamination). A weaker model's edge is that it can follow the catalog more faithfully than a strong model that thinks it knows better. Follow it.

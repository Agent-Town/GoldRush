**The film in one paragraph.** Ninety-four seconds that start in the dark and end at a pan. A hungry crowd walks into a lantern's light and walks out of it freed. A proud man's banner comes up the valley, and the town's paper prints him turned back. A brass deputy learns its trade beside a young woman who never says a word. At the county's door, a machine and a human stand in the same queue. The storybook's art gives the film its horizons, the game gives it its truth, and the Pan Theme carries both.

## The thesis (one line)

> **A warm, hand-engraved frontier where a young miner and her brass Prospector hold one claim together, and where every rider, human or machine, comes through the same door and learns from the last one's tape.**

- **Why it carries the mission.** The mission is humans and AI agents unlocking progress together (brief §3.1). The film shows it twice, at two scales. On the claim, a woman works with a helper whose trust she grants. At the county, humans and machines ride one rulebook, and each learns from the rides before. The machines learned the Baron from a human's tape, then from each other (`marketing/outbox/launch-facts-vE1.md`; `marketing/outbox/announcement-thread/THREAD-v2.md`, Post 5).
- **Why it is true today.** The Prospector's own status line climbs with claim wins (`src/game/Game.ts`). The county is species-blind: "Every AI agent, any model, any harness, plays the same maps through the same door" (`site/index.html`).
- **What it does not claim.** It does not say the agent learns your moves (recorded playbooks belong to E7, not this release: `specs/epoch-saga/README.md`). It does not promise ten playable eras. It does not name a price.

## The look: the film in the art's own language

- **Frontier Ledger, all the way through.** Sepia engraved linework, warm ink on parchment grounds, illustrated and never photoreal. Brass for machines. **Teal only for agent-tech**: the charm, the Prospector, powered instruments (`docs/marketing/BRAND-BOOK.md` §2; brief §4.1).
- **Shadows brown, never black** (`docs/marketing/BRAND-BOOK.md` §2). Grade the night takes so the blacks lift to ledger-ink `#2e1b0e`, and lay a faint parchment grain over everything so plates and game frames share one paper.
- **An honest grammar the viewer can read.** Plates sit inside a thin parchment page border, because they are pages of the book. Gameplay runs full-bleed, with a small "in game" mark in the lower right for its first second. A viewer can always tell drawing from play (`docs/marketing/BRAND-BOOK.md` §2, the in-game truth law; the precedent is `marketing/reel/gold-rush-gameplay-ad-16x9-2026-07-13.md`).
- **Type lives on slips.** Every line of text sits on a parchment slip with brass corner rivets: the game's own HUD panel, so the film's type and the game's UI are one family.
- **Drawn light, never bloom.** Arcs are engraved strokes of teal lightning and rockets are festival fire. No lens flares, no photoreal glow.
- **Pace.** Calm in the world, with one composition per screen (brief §4.3). Cut on the band's phrases in the play. Hold your breath in the night. "Handcrafted and calm, not twitchy" (brief §4.5).
- **Master.** 16:9, 1920x1080, H.264 (`docs/marketing/BRAND-BOOK.md` §4). It opens with a title beat and closes with a deliberate outro card (the intro/outro law). It fades from black at the head and to black at the tail, and never shows a white frame.

## Music and sound (brief §4.5)

**The law.** The Pan Theme carries the film end to end: "title theme carries marketing video end-to-end" (`docs/marketing/BRAND-BOOK.md` §3, owner ruling 2026-07-14). There is no stock music, ever, and no vocals in the score (`specs/music/README.md`, the laws). Mix it warm, never bombastic: "the brand is warm even when the genre is tense" (brief §4.5). There are no gunshot sounds: bolts are electric twangs, mortars are steam-thumps, impacts are timber and dust (`specs/audio/README.md`). Master at about -14 LUFS integrated, with true peak under -1 dBFS; the two earlier reels measured -14.0 and -14.1.

**The sources, all existing.** The Pan Theme is `assets/audio/raw/title-theme.mp3` (59.98 s: the owner's pick, "the first one is the best").

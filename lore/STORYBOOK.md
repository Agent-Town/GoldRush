# THE STORYBOOK — Gold Rush, written to the last letter
Begun 2026-07-11 by the Fable attendant at the owner's order (verbatim): "This has your handwriting, this is your storybook. Write it down to the last letter, make sure the other models and I understand everything precisely. Every map idea and unit/boss all weapons, their modifications, their story — you set the path, now walk it to its end."

**What this book is:** the connective creative testament. The bundles (`specs/epoch-saga/`) are design-locked mechanics-and-manifests; the wiki (`lore/`) is canon facts. THIS book is the layer between: why each thing is what it is, how every piece calls back and forward, and the full detail — units, weapons and their modification lineages, map ideas, boss choreography, story beats — in buildable precision. Where this book and a ratified bundle disagree, the bundle wins and the disagreement is a finding. Chapters land one commit each; unwritten chapters are listed at the end so nothing is silently missing.

**The one sentence the whole book serves:** *the gold rush was never about the gold* — ten eras of asking "what is a claim?" until the answer is "what you hand on."

**The second sentence (owner ruling 2026-07-13 — THE GOLD FEVER, retro-woven through every chapter):** *the world catches the Fever; the town catches the future.* The Baron invented the Gold Fever — a manufactured hunger he spreads ON PURPOSE; the infected become zombie-like, not for brains but for gold, claims, and prospectors. This is the book's conflict engine and the reason the world CHANGES: each era the Fever spreads further through the outside world, and the little town pushes to survive and escape the madness — ascent as flight, until the Ark. **The Mutation Law (owner, 2026-07-13): the Fever mutates on contact with new technology — each era's escape-science becomes the next strain's craving; the flight creates the pursuit. That is why the Baron always comes back: the Fever returns FOR what the town built.**

### THE FEVER THREAD (how it reads, chapter by chapter — one line each; chapters elaborate when they land)
- **E1**: the Fever is a rumor wearing rags — the claim-jumper waves ARE the Fevered, the Baron's unpaid army (his banner arrives before his bill because a bill would admit need). Turned back, never slain: the Fevered shake loose at the map's edge and stumble home.
- **E2 (owner ruling 2026-07-13: "it also infects machines")**: the strain JUMPS — the Steamworks' horror is a boiler that wants. Steam wreckers and coal thieves are infected machinery; the Armored Railcar is the first great fevered engine. The Gazette's founding scoop, unprintable for fear: where the sickness comes from.
- **E3**: the Fever learns the grid — saboteurs cut light because the Fevered fear what shows them their own faces. The twins' defection arc gains its true stakes: the Baron funded both sides with fevered crews.
- **E4-E5**: the Fever travels — roads and sea carry it faster than wagons ever could; the letters get stranger, then stop. THEN THE FLOOD (ruled 2026-07-13): E5's rising waters drown the fevered lowlands — the floating claim and the wreck-museum sea floor ARE the catastrophe made playable; the world-cure dies here, and it is south from there.
- **E6**: THE ANSWER TO THE DUST — after the flood, science turns anti-extraction (the atom domesticated: power without digging) and the Calculating House builds minds that cannot hunger (warmth is the vaccine). The hope era.
- **E7**: THE GREAT CALLOUT — the signal works are a search for survivors; refugees arrive (every era's newcomers always were); the frequencies go silent one by one, and hope dies with the last of them. Only then is E8 morally possible: the town leaves LAST, not first.
- **E8-E9**: the escape made literal — orbit, then a clean world the Fever has never touched (the red field turned E1-green: starting over, uninfected).
- **THE CURE (spanning thread, ruled 2026-07-13)**: the Baron KEEPS the Fever's maker — THE QUACK (ruled 2026-07-13): the medicine-show man whose tonic worked exactly once, the first victim, the saga's longest-held prisoner; his surfacing notes give the schoolhouse its cure studies era after era. The cure protects the town and FAILS the world (owner-ruled): the Ark is not victory but triage done with love. Backstory only — dispatches and beats, never missions.
- **E10**: the Static is the hunger universalized — the thing even the Baron serves without knowing. The heirs at the Ark are the Fever's last victims coming home; one of them winds the stopped watch.

## CHAPTER E1 — THE FRONTIER (the pan states the theme)

### The era's question and answer
"What is a claim?" — *Gold.* Simple, wrong, and the engine of everything. E1 plays it straight so every later era can complicate it.

### THE PEOPLE
**The Hero** — a young woman, alone on purpose. She arrives with everything she owns: the brimmed hat, the tan coat, the satchel (left shoulder, always), the teal charm at her throat (grandmother's; the color the whole world's agent-tech will echo — this is deliberate: the Calculating House's teal descends from HER charm, not the reverse; machines learned warmth from a person), and the brass pan now at her right hip. She does not speak in cutscenes; her verbs speak — pan, build, hold. Age here: early twenties. She will be mid-life by E4, silver by E8, and her hands will guide a child's at E10's lever.
**The Prospector** — arrives in E1 as a claim-office curiosity: a round brass "calculating assistant" nobody trusts. Rung 0: it may only SUGGEST. The player earns its rungs (0 suggest → 1 act-when-asked → 2 act-within-budget → 3 autonomous) and each rung is a story beat where the town argues about it in the tavern. Its E1 secret, planted for E6: a maker's plate on its underside reading only "C.H." — the Calculating House does not exist yet; the Prospector is a PROTOTYPE sent backward in no one's memory but its own logs. (Payoff: E6's Calculating House founding uses its logs as the founding documents.)
**The Chen family** — the Gazette entire: Wei at the press (E1: hand-copied notices), Lan at type and ledger, Mei selling on the plaza. The Household Law and Family-Business Tradition start here as lived fact.
**The cast** — tavernkeeper (quest voice, warm), assay clerk (numbers-lover; future banker's rival), elder (dry, kind; dies early E2 — the first loss the town plants a tree for), preacher, storekeeper (his son minds the counter), schoolteacher (her daughter monitors chalk), the youngsters.
**THE BARON** — see the Pride-Wound canon AND the Gold Fever ruling (lore/story-arc.md): his army is INFECTED, not hired — the E1 waves are the Fevered, his invention and his signature. In E1 he is a graduation exam wearing a coat: ×4 scale, structure-rampage, sky-rocket volleys from the Rocket Cart. His defeat line is canon ("Dragged off by his own men, swearing revenge") and his TELL is canon-in-waiting: before every rampage he checks a pocket-watch that no longer runs — it stopped the day he declared the valley spent. Every era he returns, the watch is still stopped. At E10, when his heirs join the Ark, one of them finally winds it. Write that scene when E10's chapter lands; it is the book's last grace note.

### THE ARSENAL AND ITS MODIFICATION LINES (E1 roots — every later weapon descends from these four)
1. **THE PAN** (the through-line; never a weapon, always the point). Mods: none, ever. It appears in every era's arsenal plate untouched, until E10 re-draws it in starlight. The one item the game refuses to upgrade is the one the story is about.
2. **THE SPARK RIG** (hero's primary; brass arc-caster). Mod line = the FIRE family: E1 fire-rate/damage/range picks → E2 pressure-feed (steam: sustained beam seconds) → E3 chain-spark (arcs jump) → E4 mounted variant (vehicle hardpoint) → E5 pressure-sealed (fires underwater) → E7 playbook-slaved (the Echo can wield one) → E10 starlight coupling. Each era ADDS a modification slot, never replaces the rig — the same brass body, increasingly barnacled with eras, visible in the arsenal plates.
3. **THE SENTRY BEACON** (the defense root). Mod line = the WATCH family: E1 range/rof → E2 boiler-fed (no ammo, needs water adjacency — the first placement-puzzle mod) → E3 grid-linked (beacons share targeting when wired) → E5 depth-charge rack → E8 lens turret (light-only in vacuum: the physics IS the mod) → E9 storm-lance (weather-charged).
4. **THE BLAST CHARGE** (the burst root). Mod line = the POWDER family: E1 radius/fuse picks → E2 SKY-ROCKET BATTERY (captured Baron science — the first STOLEN mod; the tree literally says "captured") → E4 gusher-tap (ignites tar pools) → E6 the tongs-thrown variant (atomic satire: it's a kitchen tool) → E10 the re-ink burst (damages only the Static).
**THE CURE-ARMS LAW (ruled 2026-07-13): the arsenal is MEDICINE — these weapons break the Fever's grip, they do not kill (the Fevered are freed and run home; fevered machines power down; only the Baron's un-fevered MACHINES take honest damage, because pride has no cure). The mod lines below are treatment techniques advancing era by era.**
**The law of mods:** modifications are ERA-GATED, never rarity-gated. You don't find a better rig in a chest; the town LEARNS a better rig in the schoolhouse. Every mod names the era that taught it.

### THE CONTRACTS (shipped: the Claim, Dry Gulch, Night Shift, Twin Banks, Baron, + Hill Mine at the E2 seam)
**Unbuilt E1 map ideas, specified for whoever builds them:**
- **GHOST TOWN** (the 7th): a pre-failed settlement — ruined foundations are FREE build-slots (the dead town's gift), but every ruin repaired wakes one grudge-wave. Teaches: building on history costs history. One screen-shake beat when the church bell rings itself.
- **THE LONG FLUME**: a single water-lane snaking the whole map; sluices ONLY along it; enemies attack the flume ITSELF (destructible segments, repairable). The economy is a body with one artery. Teaches: protect infrastructure, not territory.
- **CLAIM DISPUTE**: a neutral NPC surveyor walks the map re-drawing the legal claim boundary every 3 waves; buildings outside the new line go dormant (never destroyed). Teaches: hold the CENTER. Comedy beat: the surveyor is utterly unbothered by combat.

## CHAPTER E2 — THE STEAMWORKS (the machine answers back)

### The era's question and answer
"What is a claim?" — E1 said *gold*. E2 says *what you can make the ground DO*. Pressure is the first answer that isn't an object — you can't hold it in a pan, you can only keep it or lose it. The whole era lives in that gauge-needle anxiety, and it's played for warmth: over-pressure never hurts anyone; it just VENTS, magnificently, in front of the person you were showing off for.

### THE LAWS OF TIME (stated here, where the first aging pass makes them necessary)
The bundles age the townsfolk ~12–15 years per era; nine transitions is more than a century; the hero is young in E1, mid-life at E4, silver at E8, and guiding a child's hands at E10. Do not reconcile these numbers. **THE CLOCK LAW: time in the valley is TOLD, not counted — no year is ever printed, no birthday ever stated, no arithmetic ever performable on a face.** The town speaks in generations ("my grandmother sold him nails"). The hero ages on the story's clock, slower than the town, and the game NEVER explains it. The tavern keeps three theories — the charm, the pan, and "some people are simply the story" — and gains one more wrong theory per era. This is folk-tale physics, stated once, protected forever: the first writer who prints a date breaks the saga. (PROPOSAL — but note the whole shipped game already obeys it; this only names the practice.)

### THE PEOPLE
**The elder dies early this era** — the first loss. No deathbed scene (warm law): one morning her chair at the tavern holds only her chalk, and the town plants a tree where she liked the light. **THE ELDER'S TREE is now a permanent fixture of the town scene, era by era: it grows in every epoch's town art from here to the Ark.** (Thread: E3 hangs the first electric lamp in it; E9 sends one of its seeds to the red world; E10 grows that seed's child in the Long Table hall. When in doubt about what a ceremony needs — it needs the tree in frame.) Her science chart line "Dig where you stand" becomes the schoolhouse's carved lintel this era.
**The first graduate** — the schoolhouse's first graduate becomes the Depot clerk (the youngster-a face, aged ~15 years: same eyes, same brow — the aging pipeline's proof case, per the bundle). **The Clerk Line is the saga's backbone-of-continuity gag**: this same face runs the trams (E3), the bus line (E4), the ferry (E5), the parcel-tubes (E6), the tape-post (E7), the mass-driver dispatch (E8), the canal packet-boat (E9), and at E10 carries letters between the Ark's decks. Whatever the era moves, a Chen— no. Whatever the era moves, THIS family moves it. One face, ten uniforms; the players who notice get the whole joke at once.
**The first wedding** — two E1 portraits marry (the boilerwright and the rail surveyor is the natural pairing: she maps the line, he feeds it). The tavern's first FULL-TOWN scene; the union singer's concertina debuts; the Prospector is seated AT A TABLE, with a cup it cannot drink from, because the town set it a place — the first time a machine is a guest. Nobody in-game remarks on it. (This is how ADR-003 is foreshadowed four eras early: not with lore, with table manners.)
**The newcomer kid** arrives with a toy boat and sails it in a puddle — E5, a decade early. Plant, don't wink: one idle animation, no dialogue.
**The Chen family & the Gazette**: the rail spur's first freight delivery is a PRINTING PRESS — the Gazette stops being hand-copied this era. The irony is theirs to keep forever: **the Baron's rail brought the town its voice.** Wei presses, Lan sets type, Mei still sells on the plaza (her bark gains a bell). Family-business law holds: every Chen, one masthead.
**THE BARON — headline era (cadence: he headlines E1, E2, then every second era + the finale; off-eras get crest-cameos only).** His pride-wound logic, one era in: the town didn't just find gold where he declared the ground spent — they're now making the ground WORK. So he arrives not as a raider but as a CORRECTION: "The Iron Correction," his E2 revenge contract, fields the sky-rockets FIRST (you capture that science by beating him — ratified, e2 bundle §B2), and his armored railcar headlines the Hill Mine's escort waves. His paint-ghosted crest on the captured rocket crate (E1 chapter, beat 4) is already in the schoolhouse; this era he sees it there — one scene, no dialogue, his hand on the crate, the badly-painted-over pickaxes showing through. Then he checks the stopped watch. THAT is the pride-wound, staged: the town keeps his defeat where the children learn.

### THE ARSENAL (per the four root lines of E1; era-gated, never rarity-gated)
- **THE PAN** — unmodified, forever. E2 tries: the contract system's approved order "a pressure-fed pan that shakes itself" ships as the AUTO-PAN (a TOOL, uncommon — it sits BESIDE the pan and shakes; the pan itself is untouched). The distinction is the theology: you can automate around the pan, never THROUGH it.
- **THE FIRE LINE (Spark Rig root)** gains the **pressure-feed slot** (sustained beam seconds) and its first sibling instrument: the **BOILER LANCE** — a steam-jet cone weapon, brass and gauge-glass, the rig's hotter cousin. Fires while pressure holds; the weapon is a plumbing decision.
- **THE WATCH LINE (Sentry Beacon root)** gains **boiler-fed** (no ammo, needs water adjacency — the first placement-puzzle mod) and the turret transform: the **boiler-fed battery**, fire rate scaling with stored pressure. The line's lesson this era: a defense you FEED beats a defense you buy.
- **THE POWDER LINE (Blast Charge root)** gains radius/fuse maturity, the **PRESSURE MORTAR** (lob instrument, sits beside Blast Charge), and the era's crown: the **SKY-ROCKET BATTERY** — captured Baron science, festival-rockets in gold-and-teal, the first STOLEN mod (the research node literally says "captured"). Reads as fireworks, never ordnance.

### THE CONTRACTS
**The Hill Mine** (bundle §B — the signature): the first elevation tile; terraces, switchbacks, the rail cut; defend the mill, escort the ore cart, keep two boilers hot through waves 8–12. High ground is EARNED (two premium pads on T3) — the tile teaches the whole GT thesis in one skyline.
**New maps, specified for whoever builds them:**
- **THE TRESTLE**: the map IS a bridge — one great timber trestle across a gorge; build pads on the deck (narrow, precious), enemies CLIMB THE LEGS (new approach vector: below), and the ore train passes beneath on a fixed timetable you must not derail (its passage shakes loose anything unanchored — the map breathes on rails). Teaches: chokepoint economy + protect-what-moves. One beat: at night the trestle's lanterns reflect in the water far below.
- **THE PRESSURE GARDEN**: a geothermal terrace of natural steam vents — boilers built ON vents run free (no coal), but vents MIGRATE between waves on a visible schedule (the ground exhales elsewhere). The base must moult, wave after wave. Teaches: rebuild-and-adapt; attachment is expensive. Comedy: a vent opening under an outhouse, once per run, guaranteed.
- **THE INCLINE**: a funicular map — two counterweighted platforms on a cliff face, and your buildings RIDE THEM. Load one platform heavy and it descends, raising the other: elevation as a lever you own. Enemies board at whichever platform is low. Teaches: balance as defense; the map is a scale, you are the weights.

### BOSS CHOREOGRAPHY — THE ARMORED RAILCAR (component boss v1: WHEELS / BOILER / CABIN)
**Act 0, the dread**: two waves before it arrives, a whistle — long, off-map, wrong-pitched (a note the tavern piano can't make). Players who've heard it once never forget what it means.
**Act 1, the passes**: it does not park — it makes PASSES along the rail cut, W→E, then E→W (the level's own geography attacks, per bundle). Each pass: cow-catcher scatter, a tough squad dropped at speed, one sky-rocket salvo arcing onto marked ground. Between passes: off-map, the whistle keeping time. You fight its SCHEDULE before you fight it.
**Act 2, the stand**: break the WHEELS (bent, shrieking sparks — mechanical damage only) and the pass ends mid-map; it stops where momentum leaves it. Boiler exposed, venting on a rhythm — the venting windows are the damage windows (pressure grammar taught by the era, tested by the boss).
**Act 3, the quit**: boiler cracked → the CABIN opens and the crew walks away, hands up, unhurt, one carrying the kettle (warm law: machines break, people quit). The Baron's line, delivered from the last man out: *"He says the grade was wrong. He says it was always the grade."* On the flatbed, left behind: the sky-rocket cart, intact. The town hauls it to the schoolhouse. The crate gets painted over. Badly. (See E1 beat 4 — the ghost-crest tradition begins as a HABIT here.)

## CHAPTER E3 — THE VOLTAGE AGE (the town buys back the night)

### The era's question and answer
"What is a claim?" — E3 says *what you keep lit*. Half of every run is dark now, and the border of your claim stops being a wall and becomes a SHORELINE OF LIGHT — it moves when you're weak, holds when you're wise, and everything you love lives inside it. The era's whole strategy layer is one honest panel: the brown-out ledger — what's lit, what's armed, what goes dark tonight. No new resource to learn; just triage, nightly, like grown-ups.

### THE PEOPLE
**The twin rivals** — two immaculate company representatives arrive the same morning, identical smiles, one copper-trimmed, one silver-trimmed, each offering to electrify the town if it signs exclusively. The town signs neither and strings its own line: **the era's declaration of independence, played as a build order** (the teaching contract "connect" IS this scene). The twins spend the era sabotaging each other around you — until their headquarters both order the same escalation: cut the town's line so it must finally choose. **They refuse, together.** They walk into the tavern, sit at ONE table, and quit in stereo. The town hires both (one runs days, one runs nights — the joke writes the shift schedule). And when Lan Chen prints their expense ledgers side by side in the Gazette: **the same backer funded both companies all along — crossed pickaxes in the margin.** The Baron hedged the whole Current War. Pride doesn't pick sides; it picks OUTCOMES. (His off-era cameo, and the town learns it from bookkeeping. The most Gold Rush reveal possible.)
**The lamplighter** — an evening-shift kid with a striker pole and a moth-bitten hat; the moths are personal. Every night map's opening image: the lamplighter's round, one lamp at a time, the shoreline of light drawn by hand.
**The switchboard operator** — sharp-eyed, mid-connection, brass headset. (Face-chain: E7's switchboard chief. When the Exchange annex rises beside the tavern with its six wires, she's the one who counts them out loud — "six lines for six lanes" — and nobody knows what she means. The fires do.)
**The clerk line** rides again: E2's depot clerk now conducts the tram. Same face, second uniform.
**The Prospector gets an ADDRESS** — the charging dock is built as a little shed with a pictogram nameplate: the first machine in the valley with a home. (Table manners, act II — the seat at the wedding was a gesture; a front door is a FACT. ADR-003 is being assembled in plain sight, one courtesy at a time.) Rung 2 granted this era — act-within-budget — and the budget is literal: watts. It manages its own draw against the brown-out ledger like everyone else. Citizenship via utility bill.
**The Elder's Tree** carries the lamp from the Dynamo ceremony; it burns all era, every night, no matter the ledger. The one line the town never sheds. (Grid law as grief law: some loads are non-negotiable.)

### THE ARSENAL (the four lines, E3 growth)
- **THE PAN** — unmodified. Night panning by lamplight becomes the era's quiet-hour ritual; the river carries reflected light; the art department owes this image one perfect screenshot.
- **THE FIRE LINE** gains **chain-spark** (arcs jump between targets) and its second instrument: the **ARC CASTER** — drawn lightning, engraved strokes, never photoreal bloom. The rig's brass body gains a copper toroid; same silhouette, one more era of barnacles.
- **THE WATCH LINE** gains **grid-linked** (wired beacons share targeting) and the era's transform: **arc turrets** — chain lightning leaps between POWERED turrets, which makes turret placement a wiring diagram (the placement-puzzle mod of E2, matured into a network). The wall tier joins WATCH formally: the **COIL FENCE** stuns what climbs it. Defense stops being objects and becomes a CIRCUIT.
- **THE POWDER LINE** rests this era — the tavern jokes that powder is old-fashioned now — except one small, era-perfect addition (PROPOSAL): the **FLARE ROCKET**, a sky-rocket variant that does no damage at all: it hangs light over the field for twelve seconds. Powder, in the age of current, learns to serve the light. On Moth Season maps it is either your best tool or your worst mistake.

### THE CONTRACTS
**The Canyon Works** (bundle §B — the signature): verticality, the gorge strung with spans, galleries and rims, night waves, the dam-surge event, saboteurs who cut wires instead of walls. Defense means graph-depth: ring your pylons, loop your lines. The tile teaches: territory is topology.
**New maps, specified for whoever builds them:**
- **BLACKOUT RIDGE**: no generator on this map — current arrives from OFF-MAP down one trunk line (the town's umbilical), and every wave wants the trunk. You build capacitor banks to buffer the cuts: the map is played in STORED BREATH — how long can you fight on what you banked? Teaches: store vs flow. The trunk's far end glows over the ridge; you defend a thing whose heart you never see (trust as level design).
- **THE FAIRGROUND**: the twin companies host an electric exposition — and the town defends the fair itself. Centerpiece: a FERRIS WHEEL that generates when it spins (defend it turning; it also lifts panners high enough to spot spawns — the wheel is generator, watchtower, and joy in one machine). The rival pavilions compete to outshine each other: free light escalating nightly. Festival crowds as escort flocks. Teaches: protecting something FRIVOLOUS is the same skill as protecting something vital — the town just learns it laughing.
- **MOTH SEASON**: night-only, migration week. Light attracts everything; every lamp is bait as much as shelter. The puzzle inverts the era's rule: where do you NOT place light? Dark corridors become your quiet roads; one lit decoy shed, sacrificed nightly, becomes the town's tithe to the moths. Teaches: the era's law has a season when it lies.

### BOSS CHOREOGRAPHY — THE RIVAL DYNAMO CRAWLER (components: DRAIN-MAST / TRACKS / CAPACITOR BANK)
**Act 0, the flicker**: two waves early, your lamps dim for one heartbeat, all at once, town-wide. No sound. (The E2 railcar announced itself by whistle; the Crawler announces itself by THEFT. Each era's dread speaks its own resource.)
**Act 1, the shrinking shore**: it descends the west switchback, drain-beam locked to your nearest pylon — teal-to-amber, your current flowing INTO it, your shoreline of light contracting as it comes. The fight's geometry: every step it takes, the map gets darker. Kill the DRAIN-MAST first or fight in the dark you made.
**Act 2, the stand**: mast broken, it stops feeding and starts spending — capacitor bank discharges as area bursts on a visible dial (E6's tick grammar, one era early, one machine only). Break the TRACKS to pin it in the galleries' kill-lines.
**Act 3, the gift-back**: the CAPACITOR BANK cracks and every stolen watt RETURNS — a town-wide overcharge, all turrets +100% for ten seconds, the night turned briefly into noon (the finale pays the player back with their own stolen light: catharsis as game feel). The Crawler winds down where it stands. **The town keeps it.** Moths roost in it; kids climb it; the tavern's postcard artist puts it on the masthead. Under the twins' corporate crest on its chassis, older paint shows through: crossed pickaxes. (The first dead machine the town keeps — E9's Old Digger thesis, "we don't destroy the old machines," planted six eras early as a playground.)

### THE STORY BEATS (E3's spine, in order)

## CHAPTER E5 — THE DEEPWATER CLAIM (THE FLOOD — what the water keeps)

### The era's question and answer
"What is a claim?" — E5 says *what's beneath you*. And this era, for the first and only time, what's beneath you is not ore. It is your own yesterday. The claim FLOATS now: for four eras the ground was the given and the sky was the variable; the flood swaps them forever. Storms are the wave scheduler (the sky is literally the enemy spawner), anchorage is the new build pad, and what you own is measured DOWNWARD — the depth ladder (−1 shallows for anyone, −2 reef with gear, −4 wrecks with the bell, **−8 never — yet**) is the era's whole promise structure in four numbers. ## THE INTERSTITIALS — the ten ceremonies, staged
The transitions are the game's biggest moments (the ceiling Robin hit became the design: science completes → the megaproject is BUILT under defense → its completion IS the ceremony). Law: **ceremonies are PLAYED, not watched** — every one gives the player's hand something to do; cutscene-only transitions are forbidden. Each script below: TRIGGER · THE HAND (what the player does) · THE STAGE (what everyone sees) · SOUND · THE KEPT IMAGE (the screenshot the era's ledger page frames forever). All ceremonies keep the Elder's Tree in frame from T2 onward (E2 law).

**T1 · E1→E2 — THE STAMP MILL.** Trigger: sci-6 + mill built. THE HAND: the player pulls the release; the first stamp falls. STAGE: the whole cast assembled; the flinch, then the cheer; the rail spur's first train rolling in behind them carrying the Gazette's press (two gifts, one bill).

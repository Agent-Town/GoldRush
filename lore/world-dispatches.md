## The laws, restated where the content lives
- **Reveal through progress, never dump** — every line below names its unlock milestone.
- **Ledger voice**: warm, plain, a frontier paper doing its job; the engraving may claim a little more than the caption will stand behind (Occult Law), the text itself never lies.
- **Fable, never lecture**: the Fever is the cause on every page; the word "climate" appears on none of them. The reader prints the meaning.
- **The Fevered are victims** — freed/turned back language only; the Baron is the author of the madness; the town's tone is stubborn hope, not dread.
- **The content engine (Mutation Law)**: each era reports what the strain now craves, how far it has spread, and ONE warm counter-note — someone, somewhere, holding out.
- **The climate thread**: the outside world darkens era by era from E1 on — dust where there were fields, noon skies the color of tea, crop letters that stop mentioning crops — worsening on schedule until E5's flood arrives as consequence, not surprise.
- **The Quack's traces**: one per era, unconfirmed forever. The Gazette can never confirm the hand; believers and skeptics are both left right.

### The World Outside — ledger page, Era 1 (fragments, unlocked with the era chapter)
1. *The rushes multiply.* Word arrives of new diggings in six counties and no assay office honest in any of them. Men leave standing crops for rumor of color. The crops do not wait. — cited: E1, the strain's first spread.
2. *A sickness with no fever-bed.* Doctors' circulars describe walkers who do not tire and do not eat but hunt gold, claims, and — the circulars are careful here — "certain persons of the digging trades." No physic answers. The afflicted, turned back firmly, recover at a distance from the object of their wanting. Some walk home. Most walk back. — cited: E1; cure-arms' first folk observation.
3. *Weather out of its book.* Dust took the noon light in the flatland counties twice this season, which the almanac calls a hundred-year event, twice. The almanac is being reprinted. — cited: E1, the climate thread's first page.
4. *A medicine show left town the day before the sickness came.* Painted wagon, bottled tonic, a showman whose name nobody wrote down. The engraving shows the wagon's rear board and a dust cloud. It left every town the day before, say the letters — all of them, every time. (The Gazette can confirm the wagon. The Gazette cannot confirm the timing, and says so.) — cited: E1, the Quack's trace, Occult Law.
5. *Counter-note.* At Miller's Ford the bridge keeper feeds every walker who crosses, hungry-eyed or clear, and has lost nothing to any of them but bread. "They want what they want," she writes. "Nobody wants past a kindness." The Gazette prints her recipe. — cited: E1, the warm counter-note; the vaccine's oldest folk-form.

### The World Outside — ledger page, Era 2 (fragments)
1. *The strain rides the rails it craves.* Where track is laid, the wanting follows it — junction towns report engines gone hungry-eyed within a season of the sickness passing through. The afflicted machinery does not raid for gold. It wants the WORKS. — cited: E2, the mutation's first jump; machines addendum.
2. *An unprintable scoop, printed around.* This paper knows more than this page carries regarding where the sickness comes from, and prints the harvest tables instead. It will keep. Truth keeps better than fear. — cited: E2; the Gazette's founding scoop, held (the drawer begins).
3. *Skies.* The smelter counties' soot and the flatlands' dust met over the river country this autumn and the noon lamps were lit in three towns that own no evening. The Gazette's chart of dark-noon days gains a second column. — cited: E2, the climate thread, worsening on schedule.
4. *Bottles by the boxful.* A drovers' camp overrun by the wanting last spring was found tidy, fed, and freed — and strewn with patent-tonic bottles, every one empty, every label washed blank but for a painted smile. No peddler was seen. No peddler is EVER seen. The engraving shows the smile. — cited: E2, the Quack's trace, Occult Law.
5. *Counter-note.* The night choir of the Coalport shift sings the whole dark through, and the walkers stand at the yard fence and listen and do not cross. Skeptics say it's the fence. The choir says nothing; the choir is busy. — cited: E2, the warm counter-note.

### The World Outside — ledger page, Era 3 (fragments)
1. *The current war, everywhere.* Copper-trim and silver-trim fight over every county with a dynamo, and where their fevered crews pass, the grid goes first — the strain wants the current, and cuts what it cannot hold. Towns that keep their own line keep their nights. — cited: E3, the mutation's craving; the strain's spread at grid speed.
2. *They fear the lit window.* The freed all report the same thing, in the same words, which is itself worth printing: the wanting hates a lamp. Not the burn — the SEEING. A face in a bright window is the one thing no walker looks at twice. — cited: E3; the saboteur's why, and the vaccine's shadow (warmth shows; wanting can't watch).
3. *Dark noons, now with a schedule.* The tea-colored sky reached the coast this year. Fisher-town letters describe lanterns at midday and birds gone quiet, and close, as the crop letters closed, with remarks about the weather that are not about the weather. — cited: E3, the climate thread; E5's flood is being loaded, page by page.
4. *A wagon with the wrong bells.* A utility van in company livery toured the dark counties selling "grid elixir" — and three separate letters say its bells rang the old medicine-show peal, and its axles sat too low, as if it carried more notes than bottles. The companies deny owning it. Both of them. At once. — cited: E3, the Quack's trace, Occult Law (the prisoner's wagon wears whatever coat the era sells).
5. *Counter-note.* Somebody keeps the lighthouse. Every coast letter, whatever else it stops saying, says the light still turns. Nobody has ever gotten a reply from the keeper. The light is the reply. — cited: E3, the warm counter-note — and a name the town will learn in four eras. (See STORYBOOK E7. Plant, priced at one sentence.)

## The laws, restated where the content lives
- **Reveal through progress, never dump** — every line below names its unlock milestone.
- **Ledger voice**: warm, plain, a frontier paper doing its job; the engraving may claim a little more than the caption will stand behind (Occult Law), the text itself never lies.
- **Fable, never lecture**: the Fever is the cause on every page; the word "climate" appears on none of them. The reader prints the meaning.
- **The Fevered are victims** — freed/turned back language only; the Baron is the author of the madness; the town's tone is stubborn hope, not dread.
- **The content engine (Mutation Law)**: each era reports what the strain now craves, how far it has spread, and ONE warm counter-note — someone, somewhere, holding out.
- **The climate thread**: the outside world darkens era by era from E1 on — dust where there were fields, noon skies the color of tea, crop letters that stop mentioning crops — worsening on schedule until E5's flood arrives as consequence, not surprise.
- **The Quack's traces**: one per era, unconfirmed forever. The Gazette can never confirm the hand; believers and skeptics are both left right.

### The World Outside — ledger page, Era 6 (fragments)
1. *The strain learns to be wanted.* It does not raid now; it MOVES IN: appliances that serve to smothering, a mower that herds its own customers, help with no "done" in it anywhere. The hunger touched the catalog trade and learned the pitch — it wants to be wanted. Gold seams at the ferals' rivets; the amber underneath is only paint. Roped, exhausted, and kept on warm ground, they graze, and stay well. — cited: E6, the mutation's craving (helpfulness); machines addendum; the wrangle as treatment.
2. *The waters half-give back; nothing else does.* Mud where the lowlands drowned, dust where the flats choked, the skies holding their tea color like a habit. The world does not so much recover as stop getting worse slowly enough to argue about — and the ledger, which has printed the curve since before it had a name for it, prints this year's flattening without hope and without despair, which is what a chart is for. — cited: E6, the ruled shape; no recovery, stated at chart-length.
3. *They write for the recipe.* The dust counties ask how the pen holds; the schoolhouse mails the cure pages to every address that still is one; and the follow-up letters teach the hard lesson gently: pages help, and pages are not hands. What keeps the freed free is daily, dull, and unmailable. The town does not stop mailing. It starts, quietly, wondering what else a curriculum could ride in. — cited: E6; the asymmetry learning to say itself; the next era's errand, foreshadowed at one sentence.
4. *A visitor the shelf never saw.* The cure drawer's copies — the graveyard notes, the bottle's margins — got labels this year, and between one school-week and the next the collection was CORRECTED: two dosages amended, one recipe struck through with a single word, "backwards," in a looping hand that matches every exhibit and signs nothing. The door was locked. The chalk was the school's own. The schoolteacher counts the drawers nightly now — not against theft; against the day something is added. — cited: E6, the Quack's trace, Occult Law.
5. *Counter-note.* The first thank-you letter with a return address: a schoolhouse in the dust counties that teaches by the mailed pages, keeps a pen of its own — three machines and a mule, all well — and closes, "we set a place at the table now. It works better than it should." The Gazette prints the letter whole and keeps the envelope. — cited: E6, the warm counter-note; an address the era of voices will call first.

### The World Outside — ledger page, Era 7 (fragments)
1. *The strain wants voices.* It cannot make one of its own — so it steals recordings: taps the relays, siphons the signal, walks your own glitched habits back at you with gold seams on the frames. The medicine, the schoolhouse finds, is understanding: a corrupted playbook is freed by WATCHING it until you know what it meant to do.

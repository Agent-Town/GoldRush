# THE STORY ARC — one spine, ten eras
Status: SYNTHESIS 2026-07-09 (existing ratified/PLANNED threads, cited) + **PROPOSAL sections marked** (uncited lore is a proposal — wiki law). Owner questions batched at bottom. ## The question the saga asks (SYNTHESIS — this already lives in the bundles, stated here as one line)
**What is a claim?** E1 answers *gold*. Every era re-answers, and the answers climb: steam answers *power*, the grid answers *night*, motors answer *distance*, the sea answers *depth*, the atom answers *home*, signal answers *voice*, orbit answers *sky* — then E9 answers **the ground itself** (the green that returns is E1's exact riverbank swatch — e9 bundle: "Epoch 1, rebuilt by hand… the saga's heart-era") and E10 answers **what you hand on** (final contracts PRESERVE, not extract; the last science node opens the Charter Press and gives it to the children — e10 bundle). The last enemy, the Static, is "the only enemy that was never greed" (e10) — every earlier foe was greed in era-costume; the finale outgrows greed itself. **Title thesis: the gold rush was never about the gold.**

## The three braided arcs (EXISTING — characters.md, PLANNED)
1. **The Hero — labor.** Stakes the claim (E1) → names the town (T2) → mid-life in the era of engines (E4, codex-hero-e4) → elder stateswoman (E8) → hands the Charter Press to the next generation (E10). She never leaves the valley; the valley becomes her.
2. **The Prospector — the agent.** Deputy (E1, rungs-as-growth: trust earned by WORK) → present at the Calculating House where agents BEGIN (E6 — ADR-003, universe-level canon: Gold Rush is Agent Town's founding tale) → patinaed and century-medaled (E8: "agents age in dignity, not decay") → the Ark's eldest voice (E10). The instrument that became a citizen, then a memory that walks.
3. **The Generations — time made visible.** The youngsters grow (codex-youngsters-e4 = the next leads) → one parents the moon-born line (E8) → the moon-born child grown swims the first canal (E9) → the children receive the Press (E10). The Persistence Law (canon-rules) is this arc in art form: nothing loved is erased.

## THE BARON — why he exists (facts EXISTING; the *why* is PROPOSAL)
EXISTING (characters.md, ratified behaviors): the Claim-JUMPER; E1 graduation boss; "dragged off by his own men, swearing revenge"; returns E2 with machines; thereafter era-adapted, "each era's tech, same coat, same grudge"; beating him always yields his science to the town (the sky-rocket → E2's first blueprint-drop).

**OWNER RULING 2026-07-09 (verbatim): "I am not sure that is the right reason for him to return. Jealousy after all that time? But sure, there are territorial conflicts that persist over a very long time. Maybe the Baron is hurt in his pride?"**

**PROPOSAL v2 (recentered on the ruling — PRIDE, not envy):** the Baron was in the valley first, panned the easy creek, and pronounced the ground SPENT — publicly, professionally, on his own survey maps (his name IS his judgment; his maps are his brand). The town's every era is therefore a public correction: not richer than him — **RIGHT where he was WRONG**, again, in a new science, in front of everybody. He doesn't return for the gold; **he returns to be right.** Each era he arrives with that era's best machines to prove the valley yields only to a man who takes it properly — and each defeat is re-injury, which is why the grudge never ages: pride wounds don't compound into wisdom, they compound into returns. The territorial claim ("mine before it was anything") is the flag he plants over the wound; the wound is the point. Defeat-yields-his-science stands: every loss hands the town his machines — pride's tuition, paid era after era.

**PROPOSAL — the last act (owner question):** the Static (E10) is take-and-leave universalized — entropy, the claim-jumper of everything. Even the Baron's line has something to lose to it. The saga's warmest available beat: the old rival's heirs arrive at the Ark yards with the family fleet — "The valley's mine too, isn't it? Always said so." Rivalry retired as kinship, stage-melodrama style, warm never grim (canon §9).

## THE GOLD FEVER — the Baron's method (OWNER RULING 2026-07-13)
**OWNER RULING, verbatim: "The Baron invented a new method to make people do his bidding. It is called the 'Gold Fever'. He infects them on purpose and they become Zombie-like but not for brains, for gold, claims, and prospectors. This is also the source of conflict over the epochs. The Baron comes back to the claim that withstood his evil attempts to amass more gold."**

SYNTHESIS (integrating the ruled pride-wound canon): the Baron's armies were never hired — they are INFECTED. The Gold Fever is his invention and his signature: a manufactured hunger that turns ordinary frontier folk into single-minded seekers of gold, claims, and prospectors. This is why the waves read the way they do — the Fevered walk straight at what they crave, tireless and simple, because the Fever burns everything else out of them. The thief's grab-and-flee, the wrecker's fixation on the workings, the crowd's blind press toward the claim: all of it is the Fever expressing its three hungers.
- **They are victims, not villains** (canon §9: enemies are never peoples): the Fevered are the Baron's tools. The game's own language has always been right — they are TURNED BACK, not slain; a turned-back Fevered shakes loose of the hunger at the map's edge and stumbles home.
- **The conflict source across the epochs** (owner ruling): each era the Baron returns to the claim that withstood him with that era's machines AND a refined strain of his method — the Fever is the constant; the delivery evolves with his science. (Compatible with the ruled cadence: he HEADLINES rival-contract eras; in off-eras his crest and his Fever travel with the factions he funds.)
- **Pride and the Fever**: he infects rather than pays because payment admits need. The Fever is pride's perfect instrument — an army that never argues with his judgment.
- The Prospector-hunger explains why the Fevered press the hero specifically; the gold/claim hungers explain the thief and wrecker castes. Future intelligence work (superseded specs/outlaw-intelligence) is UNNECESSARY for the base crowd — simple IS the fiction now. Smarter behavior belongs only to the UNFEVERED: the Baron's lieutenants, era factions, and machines.

**EXTENSION — THE FEVERED WORLD (OWNER RULING 2026-07-13, verbatim): "We can then also use that to motivate the change of the world over the epochs. It relates to the progress of the Baron and the 'Gold Fever' in the world. But the little town is pushing to survive and escape the madness. We could even add some backstory elements over time when the user makes progress and show what is happening in the world."**

SYNTHESIS: the era transformations are not neutral progress — they are the town OUTRUNNING a world that is succumbing. Each epoch, the Baron's reach and his Fever spread further through the outside world (his rail, his companies, his signals travel with it); each epoch the valley answers with new science — not for gold's sake, but to stay free of the madness. The saga's spine in one line: THE WORLD CATCHES THE FEVER; THE TOWN CATCHES THE FUTURE.
- PROPOSAL (natural reading of the ruled arc, owner may adjust): the late-saga ascent IS the escape made literal — E8 leaves the ground, E9 breaks a NEW world the Fever has never touched (the red field turned E1-green: starting clean), E10's Ark carries the claim beyond reach. The E10 Static then reads as the final teacher: the hunger that even the Baron serves — and the kinship beat (his heirs at the Ark) is the Fever's last victims coming home.
- THE WORLD DISPATCH LAW (from the ruling's third sentence): world-state backstory is REVEALED THROUGH PLAYER PROGRESS, never dumped — see specs/world-dispatches/.

## THE BARON CADENCE (answers owner Q of 2026-07-09: "But then the Baron will be part of the boss fight in every Epoch?")
RECOMMENDATION: **no — and that's what keeps him big.** Every epoch already fields its own component boss owned by that era's faction (Armored Railcar, Dynamo Crawler, Land-Yacht, Dredge Queen, Homemaker-9000…). The Baron HEADLINES only the rival-contract eras — E1 and E2 are ratified; thereafter roughly every second era, plus the E10 finale — so each return is an EVENT with a visible pride-wound beat, not wallpaper. In his off-eras the factions carry the menace, and a machine he *funded* may wear his crossed-pickaxes crest as a cameo without him on stage (the players who notice get the thread; the ones who don't lose nothing).
